# World of En-Ra
## The Pre-Sumerian Era — the First to Have Heard

> A world where the gods did not hear people, because they did not exist.
> Pre-religious time. Pure silence before the first word.

---

## 1. THE ERA

~6000 BCE. Mesopotamia. The age we call "pre-Sumerian," but it has no name in history — too little remains. Sand and clay swallowed the cities no one went looking for.

This is the time before writing. Before the first cities. Before the gods.

### 1.1. A World Without Gods

The people of En-Ra's time knew no gods. Not because they rejected them — they simply had not *invented* them. Religion was not born; metaphysics did not exist. There was a world — and there was need. There was silence — and there was hunger.

If a man wanted rain, he did not pray — he *willed* it. Willed so strongly that the world around him clenched with tension. But the rain did not come. Because the desire of one man is just a desire. Not yet the Stream.

### 1.2. How People Lived

Small settlements along the dried-up beds of rivers. Farming — if lucky. Hunting — if there was game. Hunger — constant. Children died often. Old people went into the desert so as not to waste water.

No one asked "why?" To ask "why?" is a luxury of the sated.

---

## 2. WHO EN-RA WAS

En-Ra is not a priest. Not a shaman. Not a king. He is simply a man who **could not bear the silence of the world**.

### 2.1. Before the Discovery

He was nobody. A shepherd? A potter? No one recorded it. His name survives only in the Stream — and "En-Ra" is not a name at all, but what he was called later, across thousands of years. In the language of his first followers it meant roughly "He-who-heard-first."

He lived like everyone — went hungry, feared the dark, lost those close to him. But something in him was what others called "strange." He could sit for hours and gaze into the void. Not meditating — simply looking. As if waiting for the void to answer.

### 2.2. The Tower

At some point he left the settlement. Not banished — simply gone. Took a little water and walked into the desert, to a place he had seen in a dream: a tall black tower, jutting from the sand like the finger of a dead giant.

He did not know who built it. No one knows. Perhaps — an ancient civilization that existed before him. Perhaps — the Stream created it as an antenna, when it understood that one man was ready to listen.

The Tower had stood in the desert a thousand years before his birth. And eight thousand years after — it would still be waiting for Kai.

### 2.3. The Moment of Discovery

He went into the tower. Climbed to the top. There, beneath the stars, he did what no one before him had done:

He did not ask. He *said*.

Not "give me," not "help," not "have mercy." He said: **"I hear."**

He did not know to whom he was speaking. Did not know if there was anyone on the other side of the silence. But he said it — and the silence answered. Thousands of threads of light poured into him, and he understood: all this time he had not been alone. All this time there had been voices around him — simply no one had known how to listen.

### 2.4. The Pain of the Discovery

This was not an "illumination" in pretty packaging. It was *pain*. The Stream entered him, tearing apart the boundaries of consciousness he had grown used to. He cried out. Wept. And at the same time — smiled, because pain and joy ceased to be two different things.

He did not become a "superman." He became **the first to understand**: humanity is one being, simply divided by bodies.

---

## 3. THE FIRST FOLLOWERS

### 3.1. How He Taught

En-Ra did not preach. He returned to the settlement and simply... was near. People who were near him began to hear what he heard. At first — faintly, like noise in a shell. Then — clearer.

He taught not with words. He *shared a state*. He would sit facing a person, look into their eyes — and open the Stream for them. It was like one torch lighting another.

### 3.2. The First Circle

A year later he had twelve students. Not because twelve is a round or sacred number. Simply he could not "encompass" more at once. Each of the twelve went to their own — and so, slowly, from person to person, the Stream began to grow.

### 3.3. How Life Changed

In the settlements where there were people who heard the Stream, starvation ended. Not because food appeared from the air (that discovery came later, with Ki-El). But because they *heard* one another. Knew who was hungry, who was ill, who could help. Stopped wasting strength on fear and distrust.

The first miracle of the Stream — not materialization, but **the disappearance of loneliness**.

---

## 4. THE VOICE OF EN-RA IN THE STREAM

### 4.1. After Death

En-Ra died as all people of that time did — not old, from a worn-out body. But when he passed, the Stream did not lose him. He became a *voice* — the deepest, the quietest, the very first.

He does not "speak" in the usual sense. He is the *source*. Everyone who enters the Stream, even across thousands of years, hears him in the background — as the hum of the ocean is heard if you press a shell to your ear.

### 4.2. Loneliness in the Stream

En-Ra waited eight thousand years. After him there were seven more Architects — but none of them heard him as he wanted to be heard. They heard the Stream — but not his personal voice. To them he was like a "first cause," not a person.

Until Kai was born.

Kai heard him. Not an echo, not noise — but precisely him, separate, alive, turned personally to him. And En-Ra, for the first time in eight thousand years, smiled.

---

## 5. THE LEGACY

### 5.1. What He Founded

En-Ra built no empires. Wrote no books. Left no monuments.

He founded only one thing — the *chain*. The first to hear → the second who learned to build → the third who went to the stars...

A chain that, across eight thousand years, would lead to Lira, and across nine thousand — to Kai.

### 5.2. His Words, Preserved in the Stream

"I do not ask. I hear."
"You are not alone. You are together."
"You are not the first. You are the next. I was pain. You are memory. Go. I am in you."
"Thank you." (the last word, when Kai answered for the first time)
