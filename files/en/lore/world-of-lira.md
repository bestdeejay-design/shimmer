# World of Lira
## The Civilization of the Stream — 8,000 Years of Unbroken Development

> A world where there are no machines — there are dreams.
> No factories — there is collective imagination.
> No religion — there is knowledge.
> And no death — there is the Shimmer.

---

## 1. GENERAL DESCRIPTION

A civilization built on **technologies of consciousness**. Eight thousand years of unbroken development — without dark ages, without loss of knowledge, without wars (at least, not in the usual sense). Each generation gains access to all the experience of those before — directly, through the Psi-Stream.

People do not remember when it was otherwise. The Stream is as much a part of their world as gravity or air.

### Key Principles

- **Every person is born connected** to the Psi-Stream. The connection need not be established — it must not be lost.
- **Knowledge is not something one seeks. One breathes it.** Everything ever discovered is available to anyone who knows how to listen.
- **Energy is desire.** The stronger the collective intention, the more real its embodiment.
- **Matter is frozen dream.** Any object, building, world is a dream that has become dense.
- **There is no death. There is transition.** The body dies — the consciousness continues to shimmer in the Stream.

---

## 2. HOW SOCIETY IS ORGANIZED

### 2.1. Social Structure

No hierarchy in the usual sense. No kings, councils, parliaments. There is only the **Architect** — and this is not a ruler. The Architect is a tuning fork. He sets the tone, the direction, the emotional palette of the millennium.

Every person is free. But everyone hears everyone. This is not a limitation — it is the foundation: your freedom ends where another's pain begins, and you *feel* this directly.

### 2.2. Economy

No money. No trade. Everything a person needs is created on demand through the Stream:
- Food — materializes from the collective desire to be sated.
- Dwelling — grows from the form imagined by architects and builders.
- Tools — synthesized for the task.

The "payment" for anything is attention and gratitude to the Stream. The more you give to the Stream (dreams, knowledge, creativity), the more the Stream gives you.

### 2.3. Children

Children are born only from love. A conscious choice of two lovers — to create a new memory, a new voice in the Stream. A child hears its parents from birth — not in words, but in feelings. It knows it is loved before it learns the meaning of the word "love."

Children are taught not by teachers in classrooms, but by **mentors** — those who feel that the child precisely needs their knowledge. A lesson is not a lecture, but an immersion: the mentor shares a memory, and the student lives it as their own.

### 2.4. Attitude Toward the Body

The body is temporary. 30–100 years — and a person "goes to shimmer." This is not met with tragedy. Death is the next stage, like growing up. A teenager awaits adulthood; an adult awaits the Shimmer.

No one strives for bodily immortality. Why? When your consciousness will sound in the Stream forever, the body becomes simply a tool that one day wears out.

### 2.5. The Memory of People

If a person has no children — they are remembered by friends, students, those they helped. But children are the strongest continuation. "You will live as long as you are remembered" — in Lira's world this is not a metaphor. Memory in the Stream is real power.

## 3. TECHNOLOGIES OF CONSCIOUSNESS (PSI-TECHNOLOGIES)

### 3.1. Psi-Construction

Cities are not built — they are *imagined*. A group of architects (in our sense — designers) creates the image of a building in the finest detail. The more people connect to this image, the faster the building takes physical form.

The material is not concrete or steel. It is **frozen dream** — a substance that looks like stone or metal, but is in essence condensed intention. It can be melted back into the Stream if the building is no longer needed.

### 3.2. Psi-Terraforming

Climate changes not by machines — but by will. If colonists want rain on a new planet, they imagine rain. All together. Thousands, millions of consciousnesses, synchronously willing the same thing. And the rain falls.

Oceans, forests, mountains — all of this can be created or changed by collective imagination.

### 3.3. Psi-Navigation

To travel between the stars, no ships are needed. One must *imagine oneself at another point*. Consciousness moves instantly — the body follows, if the Stream is strong enough.

For long journeys (to new worlds), "threads" are used — trails left by pioneers in the Stream. If at least one person has reached a distant star and "anchored" there, anyone in the Stream can step along that thread.

### 3.4. Psi-Synthesis

New materials, plants, forms of life — all of this is synthesized from the Stream. The recipe: imagine what does not exist, with such force that the Stream believes in its necessity.

The discovery of new materials is not laboratory work, but art. A synthesis-artist imagines a new kind of wood that could grow in a vacuum — and after thousands of attempts the Stream "answers."

### 3.5. Psi-Archivation

In the Stream is stored all the knowledge of the civilization. Access — on request. Want to know what a planet looked like before terraforming? — "Ask" the Stream to show you.

The problem: the more knowledge, the harder it is to find. Architects introduce structure, but without an Architect the Stream becomes too "loud" — too many voices, information unfiltered.

## 4. THE CITIES OF LIRA'S WORLD

### 4.1. General Aesthetics

Cities have no "style" in our sense. Each city is the **frozen dream** of its inhabitants. Therefore they look like dreams — fluid, illogical, beautiful.

- Buildings have no straight angles — they are smooth, like hills.
- The material is translucent — the walls glow from within.
- Colors change depending on the mood of the inhabitants.
- Streets can flow, like rivers — in the literal sense.

## 5. THE PROBLEM: A WORLD THAT FORGOT HOW TO NEED

A civilization on the wane. Everything is created. Everything is decided. No hunger — food materializes. No disease — the body is healed by the Stream. No death — there is the Shimmer. No unknown — all knowledge is available.

And precisely for this reason the civilization **loses its edge**.

People stop dreaming with the same force, because dreams come true too easily. The Stream grows dimmer. Shimmers — shorter. The bond between generations — thinner.

Lira feels this more acutely than anyone. She is the last Architect at the close of the era. Her world is tired. It needs someone who does **not know** what ease is. Someone who will learn to dream through pain, thirst, and loneliness.

She needs Kai.

## 6. EVERYDAY LIFE (MORNING OF A GIRL IN LIRA'S WORLD)

The child wakes not from an alarm, but because the room begins to glow — softly, warmly, in the rhythm of her breathing. The walls change color from deep blue to gold — the city is waking with her.

She does not hear sounds — she feels: mother is already awake, she is preparing breakfast not on a stove, but by imagining it — and the food appears on the table hot, because mother imagined it hot.

Beyond the window — not a street, but a space. Other houses float in the air, connected by bridges of light. People do not walk — they glide, because why walk, if one can move by the force of thought.

The girl steps out of the house — and feels the world. Every tree, every bird, every passerby — all are part of one field. She does not know the word "loneliness" — it has no meaning, when you always hear everyone who loves you.

---

## 7. THE OPPOSITE (MORNING OF A BOY ON A DISCONNECTED PLANET)

The boy wakes from the cold. The dawn is grey. The walls — fired clay. No light, except the dim glow of the hearth.

He does not hear his mother — he sees her. She is already at the well, drawing water. There is little water — for everyone. He knows: today he will work for his food.

Beyond the window — the desert. Nothing but sand, stone, and mirage. He has no one to talk to, except himself. He does not know what it is to "feel another at a distance." For him, feelings are words that must be spoken aloud.

He hears only the wind. And sometimes — at night, when he falls asleep — something else. Something he has no name for.
